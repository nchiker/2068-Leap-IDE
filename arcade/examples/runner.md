# Leap Runner, four levels (`runner.arc`)

`platformer.arc` (the one-level reference: `platformer.md`) with **three more worlds**: the example of slice B5 of the banked cartridge map
(`docs/ARCADE_BANKING_CONTRACT.md` section 10). The game is larger than the 16 KB game area of a cartridge: the four 128 x 16 worlds are `MAP`s that
`WORLD` loads, so the linker puts them in the **banked part** of the cartridge (DOCK chunk 2, read through the window at `$4000`), and the mapped part
(code, data, sprites, the window's tables) is what stays in the 16 KB. The game is not a toy of the banking: with every map forced `mapped` it does
**not build** (16,772 of 16,384 bytes); banked it builds as one DCK of chunks 0 1 2 4 5 (40,969 bytes) whose mapped part is 16,131 of 16,384 bytes (253 to spare).
Since slice T5 of the bankable tables (`docs/ARCADE_TABLES_CONTRACT.md`) the level data are four `TABLE`s, not initialisers in the code: the linker banks the
worlds and then, as far as the mapped part needs it, the tables (here `lv1`; with `bankAll` all four), and a fifth level costs one more table record, which is bankable.
Before T5 the mapped part was 16,342 of 16,384 (42 to spare) with only the four worlds banked (1,670 bytes), and the program's variable block held 711 slots (1,422 bytes) against 376 (752 bytes) now.

## Files

| File | What |
|---|---|
| `runner.arc` | the game: `platformer.arc`'s one `SCENE` (title / playing / level complete / game over) with the level code described below |
| `runner.assets.gen.mjs` | level 1 is the platformer's world (`platformer.assets.gen.mjs` is imported, not copied); levels 2-4 are laid out here; writes `runner.assets.json`; `--ascii` prints the four levels, `--data` the level data (the `DIM`s and `TABLE lv1` .. `lv4`), `--splice` writes them into `runner.arc` between its `@@DATA-BEGIN@@` / `@@DATA-END@@` markers |
| `runner.assets.json` | generated, committed (art, sounds and tiles are the platformer's; four worlds `pf_world`, `rn_world2` .. `rn_world4`) |
| `runner.script.json` | generated by `node tests/arcade-runner.mjs --record`: a recorded play-through, title to win screen (1,762 ticks, two of the three lives lost on the way), as joystick-1 stick values per tick; `runner.script.<N>.json` are the four per-level searches it is chained from |

## Build

```
node arcade/tools/arcadec.mjs arcade/examples/runner.arc --assets arcade/examples/runner.assets.json --runtime v3
```

Needs the v3 runtime build with the banked cartridge map (`RT_LOADMAPPTR`, `RT_WORLDLOAD`) and `RT_TABLELOAD`. The compile result's `usage.mapped` and `usage.banked`
report the placement (`arcadec` prints the older usage lines only). Controls are the platformer's.

## What changed from the platformer

* `MAP level / level2 / level3 / level4` and `WORLD levelN` in `LOADLEVEL`: the world is loaded, the camera goes to 0, the window is redrawn (one
  long tick; the title, the start of a game and every level change take it).
* **The level data is per level, in four tables**: `TABLE lv1` .. `lv4` (not `level1` .. : those names are the `MAP`s) each hold the lists of the platformer (`CXS CYS SX SY WLO WHI CKX CKY CIX RCF RNX`) for
  that level alone, **indexed from 0**, and the two counts `CE` / `SE` (its coins / enemies), which replace the old absolute indexes and the `LCB` / `LSB` starts and `CB` / `SB`. The arrays are sized to the largest level
  (50 coins, 11 enemies); `LOADLEVEL` does `LOADTABLE lv` (the index form: `lv` 0..3 is the table's place in the source) and the table zeroes what a shorter level does not list; `LV` is the level
  (0..3), the taken / killed flags `CC` / `SD` are per level and indexed from 0 as well. Everything else (the physics, the enemies, the coins that spin by tile swaps, the camera) is the platformer's, line for line.
  Two small economies the variable space and the 16 KB asked for: the walker / bat type is no longer a list (a bat is an entry with no turning points, `WHI = 0`), and `CIX` has an entry per 128 px (it had one per 64).
* **The flag** (`WIN`): after levels 1 to 3 the state is 4, "LEVEL n" goes up (n = the next level), about 1.8 s later `LOADLEVEL` loads that
  world (`WORLD levelN`), the message is cleared and the level starts at its own start (x 24, three checkpoints, the HUD shows the level and its coin
  total); after level 4 the win screen ("YOU FINISHED LEAP RUNNER!"), fire goes back to the title.
* **Losing**: the lives (3) and the score belong to the game, not the level; a hurt or a fall goes back to the level's last checkpoint as before. When
  the last life goes, "GAME OVER"; fire then **restarts the current level** (`WORLD` of the same level: "LEVEL n", three lives, the score it
  started the level with).

## The levels

All four are 128 x 16 (rows 0-1 mountains, 2-4 clouds, 5-15 the gameplay band), start at x 24 on the ground, have three checkpoints and the flag at
column 122, and use the platformer's tileset. The hero's reach is the platformer's (a full jump rises 36 px and covers about 42; the layouts keep
to pits of at most 4 cells, gaps between islands of at most 3, steps and platforms of at most 3, walls of 2 and spikes 2 wide, and a brick platform
leaves 3 free rows under it or is a plank).

| | Level | Coins | Enemies | What it asks |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Meadow (the platformer's level) | 50 | 7 walkers, 4 bats | pits, one island crossing, spikes, a wall |
| 2 | Ridge | 24 | 5 walkers, 3 bats | hills, three pits, spikes and a two-high wall, planks over the hills |
| 3 | Islands | 22 | 5 walkers, 4 bats | two long pits crossed on islands and planks, bats over them |
| 4 | Gauntlet | 27 | 5 walkers, 5 bats | strips of three between pits, a spike row to hop over, narrow islands, walls, low bats, the most enemies |

## Tests and checks

`node tests/arcade-runner.mjs` (about 15 s; `ARCADE_QUICK=1` stops the runtime runs after the first level change): the data (assets, the `DIM` and `TABLE` lines, the maps against the lists, the walkers' turning points by a second rule), the build (mapped part,
the four banked worlds, the does-not-fit error with every map forced `mapped`), the recorded play-through of all four levels on the reference interpreter,
the same on the real runtime in TSRun tick by tick through every level change, the normal build against `bankAll` (state and picture), and a negative control.
`arcade/tools/xemu/runner-*` compare the cartridge on TSRun, ZEsarUX 13.0 and Fuse 1.9.1 over a scenario with level changes (the order of the commands is in
the header of `runner-compare.mjs`).
